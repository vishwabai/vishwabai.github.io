Orientation

Why data analysis comes before the model

Every ML/DL model is a function of the data you feed it. Bad distributions, leaked features, or unscaled inputs will sink a good architecture faster than a bad architecture sinks good data. This lab walks the full pipeline, basic to advanced, with something to drag, click, or watch move in every module.

raw data → cleaned → engineered → model-ready

The pipeline you're about to walk

Types→ Stats→ Distributions→ Skew/transform→ Hypothesis tests→ EDA charts→ Correlation→ Missing data→ Outliers→ Leakage→ Scaling→ Encoding→ Binning→ Selection→ PCA→ Splits/CV→ Bootstrap→ Imbalance→ Anomalies→ DL data→ Text/TF-IDF→ Time series→ Augmentation→ Drift

Garbage in

A model trained on leaked, unscaled, or biased data will look fine in validation and fail in production. Most "model" bugs are actually data bugs.

This lab's rule

Every concept gets a live widget. You change a parameter, the output changes immediately — that's the fastest way to build intuition for distributions and transforms.

Why it matters downstream

Numerical columns feed directly into scalers. Categorical columns need encoding before any linear model or neural net can use them — trees can sometimes handle raw categories, but gradient-based models (including every deep net) need numbers. Get the type wrong and every step after it compounds the error.

Foundations · 02

Descriptive statistics

Mean, median, std, and skew are the four numbers that tell you the shape of a column before you've plotted anything. Add or remove an outlier below and watch which statistics move and which don't.

live · click canvas to add a point, double-click to remove nearest
—

Mean vs median

Mean is pulled toward outliers; median isn't. If mean ≫ median, you have right-skew — usually means log-transform before modeling.

Std & variance

Std is the average distance from the mean. High std relative to mean (high coefficient of variation) often signals a column that needs scaling before use in distance-based or gradient-based models.

Skewness tells you if the tail is longer on one side (right-skew > 0, left-skew < 0). Kurtosis tells you how heavy the tails are relative to a normal distribution — high kurtosis means more extreme outliers than a normal curve would predict.

Hypothesis testing

Before trusting that a feature actually matters, a statistical test tells you whether an observed difference could plausibly be noise. Pick a test and watch the test statistic update against two sample groups.

live · compare two groups, regenerate samples
—
TestComparesUse on
t-testmeans of 2 groupsnumeric feature across a binary category
Chi-squarefrequency counts across categoriestwo categorical columns — are they independent?
ANOVAmeans across 3+ groupsnumeric feature across a multi-class category

A small p-value (conventionally <0.05) means the observed gap is unlikely under pure chance — it's evidence the feature carries signal, not proof the effect is large or causal.

Visual EDA · 05

Correlation & multicollinearity

Drag points to reshape the cloud and watch the Pearson correlation coefficient update live. High correlation between features (not target) is multicollinearity — it destabilizes linear model coefficients.

live · drag the orange points
—

Pearson vs Spearman

Pearson catches linear relationships. Spearman uses ranks — it catches monotonic but non-linear relationships Pearson misses.

VIF (Variance Inflation Factor)

VIF > 5–10 on a feature means it's largely explainable by other features. Drop it or regularize (L1/L2) before fitting a linear model.

Cleaning · 07

Outlier detection

Toggle the detection method and watch the boundary lines move — points outside the boundary turn red. Different methods disagree, especially on skewed data.

live · IQR vs Z-score boundaries
—

IQR method: flag points outside [Q1 − 1.5·IQR, Q3 + 1.5·IQR]. Robust to skew, doesn't assume normality.
Z-score method: flag points where |x − mean| / std > 3. Assumes roughly normal data — unreliable on skewed columns since the mean/std are themselves pulled by the outliers.

Cleaning · 11

Data leakage

Leakage is when information from outside the training set — often from the future, or from the test set itself — sneaks into training. It's the single most common reason a model looks great in development and fails in production.

click each leakage pattern

Target leakage

A feature that is itself derived from the target, or only known after the outcome occurs — e.g. "was_refunded" when predicting "is_fraud." The model learns a shortcut that won't exist at prediction time.

PatternLooks likeFix
Target leakagefeature only exists after the outcome happenedtrace every feature's timestamp relative to the prediction point
Temporal leakagerandom split lets the model see future rows during trainingsplit chronologically; validate on a strictly later time window
Preprocessing leakagescaler/encoder/imputer fit on the full dataset before splittingfit transforms on train only, then apply to val/test
Duplicate-row leakagethe same (or near-identical) row appears in both train and testdedupe before splitting, group-aware splitting for repeated entities

Scaling & normalization

Gradient descent (every neural net) and distance-based models (KNN, SVM, K-means) are sensitive to feature scale. Watch the same point cloud get squeezed or stretched by each method.

—
MethodFormulaUse when
Min-Max(x−min)/(max−min)bounded inputs needed (e.g. image pixels, sigmoid inputs)

Feature engineering · 11

Dimensionality reduction — PCA

PCA finds the axis along which your data varies the most, and rotates the dataset onto it. Drag the slider to rotate the candidate axis and watch the projected variance bar respond — the angle that maximizes it is the first principal component.

live · rotate axis, maximize variance captured
—

PC1 is the rotation angle that captures the most variance; PC2 is perpendicular to it. Projecting onto just PC1 reduces 2D data to 1D while keeping as much spread (information) as possible. In practice PCA runs on many dimensions and you keep enough components to explain ~90–95% of variance.

ML/DL-specific · 18

Bootstrapping & confidence intervals

A single metric (accuracy = 0.84) hides how much that number would wobble on a different sample. Bootstrapping resamples your data with replacement to build a distribution of the metric itself.

live · click resample, watch the distribution build
—

Each click draws n rows with replacement from the original sample and recomputes the mean. After enough draws, the spread of those means gives you a 95% confidence interval — the range you'd expect the true mean to fall in, without assuming a normal distribution.

Data for deep learning — images & text

Tabular rules still apply, but images and text need their own preprocessing before they're "model-ready." Toggle below to see pixel normalization and token embeddings.

live · image pixel normalization
—

Image pipeline

Resize → normalize pixel values → (optionally) standardize per-channel using dataset mean/std → augment. CNNs train faster and more stably on normalized inputs.

Text pipeline

Raw text → tokenize (subword/BPE) → map tokens to integer IDs → embedding lookup turns IDs into dense vectors the network can actually use. Sequence length gets padded or truncated to a fixed size for batching.

A time series is one signal made of several overlapping ones — trend, seasonality, and noise. Toggle each component to see what's left when you remove it; this decomposition is the basis for most forecasting feature engineering.

ConceptMeaning

Dataset shift & drift

A model is only as good as the assumption that production data looks like training data. Drag the slider forward in time and watch the live distribution drift away from the training distribution it was fit on.

—
Type of shiftWhat changes
Covariate shiftinput feature distribution changes, the relationship to the target doesn't
Concept driftthe relationship between features and target itself changes
Label shiftclass proportions change (e.g. fraud rate rises) while feature distributions per class stay stable

Detecting drift means continuously comparing live feature distributions against the training baseline (e.g. with population stability index or KL divergence) and re-triggering the full EDA → retrain pipeline when they diverge.

Production · 26

End-to-end EDA checklist

The order matters — doing scaling before splitting, or imputing before checking missingness patterns, silently leaks information. This is the sequence that keeps your pipeline honest.

  • Shape & types — row/column count, dtype of every column, memory footprint.
  • Missingness — % missing per column, is it random (MCAR) or structured (MNAR)?
  • Descriptive stats — mean/median/std/skew per numeric column, value counts per categorical.
  • Visual pass — histograms for shape, box plots for outliers, scatter/pairplot for relationships.
  • Correlation & leakage check — drop features that are proxies for the target, flag multicollinearity.
  • Train/test split first — before any fitting of scalers, encoders, or imputers.
  • Fit transforms on train only — scaling, encoding, imputation params come from train, applied to val/test.
  • Handle imbalance — only on the training set, never on validation/test.
  • Feature selection / PCA — reduce dimensionality once leakage-safe splits exist.
  • Sanity-check the final tensor — no NaNs, expected shape, expected value range, before it touches the model.
