Newton's Method

Newton's method uses the tangent line to iteratively zoom in on a root. Starting from guess x₀, each step replaces it with the x-intercept of the tangent — converging quadratically fast.

🎯 Quick Check

Newton's method fails when f'(xₙ) ≈ 0. What does this look like geometrically?
Tangent is nearly horizontal → x-intercept flies off to ±∞

The function is discontinuous there
A near-zero derivative means the tangent line is almost flat. Its x-intercept (the next guess xₙ₊₁) shoots far away. This is why starting near a local extremum causes Newton's method to diverge.

Bisection Method

Bulletproof but slow. Uses the Intermediate Value Theorem: if f(a)·f(b)<0, a root lies between them. Each step halves the interval — watch the squeeze animation.

vs Newton: Bisection always converges (linear rate). Newton converges faster (quadratic) but can fail. Real systems often combine both: bisect first to get a safe bracket, then switch to Newton.

🎯 Quick Check

The harmonic series diverges, yet its terms→0. Which statement is correct?
Terms→0 is necessary but not sufficient for convergence
This contradicts the divergence test

The divergence test says aₙ↛0 IMPLIES divergence. But aₙ→0 does NOT imply convergence — the harmonic series is the classic counterexample. You need a stronger test (integral test, comparison, etc.).

Arc length sums infinitely many tiny straight segments. As each shrinks, the sum becomes ∫√(1+f'²) dx — Pythagoras applied to the infinitesimal.

Arc Length Formula
L = ∫[a,b] √(1 + [f'(x)]²) dx
Each tiny segment: √(dx²+dy²) = √(1+(dy/dx)²) dx — Pythagorean theorem!

🎯 Quick Check

A circle of radius r has curvature κ = ?

κ=1/r for a circle. A tiny circle (small r) bends sharply (large κ). A huge circle approaches a straight line (κ→0). The osculating circle at any curve point IS the best-fit circle there.

Live Optimization Ball
0.10

Gradient Descent Update
xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − η·f'(xₙ)
η too large → overshoots & diverges. η too small → painfully slow.
Machine Learning: In neural networks, f is the loss, x is millions of weights, and this rule (with gradients) updates them all. Adam, RMSProp, and other optimizers are clever extensions of this same idea.

🎯 Quick Check

Lagrange Multipliers

∇f = λ·∇g   and   g(x,y) = c
At the optimum: level curves of f are tangent to the constraint curve

🎯 Quick Check

Maximize f=xy on x+y=10. Lagrange gives y=λ and x=λ so x=y. Answer?
x=y=5, max f=25 (AM-GM equality!)
x=10, y=0

∇f=(y,x), ∇g=(1,1). Lagrange: y=λ, x=λ → x=y. With x+y=10: x=y=5, max=25. This proves AM-GM: xy ≤ ((x+y)/2)² with equality when x=y.
